\begin{abstract}
Rapid global urbanization creates urgent challenges in zoning compliance, green cover preservation, transportation infrastructure, and spatial resilience. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{UrbanGen AI}, an edge-deployable multi-model generative framework for aerial remote sensing analysis and municipal urban planning decision support. To ensure rigorous, fair comparative benchmarking, all core models are trained under a uniform, controlled 50-epoch protocol on the UCMerced LandUse benchmark: (i) a U-Net Denoising Autoencoder achieving 29.65\,dB PSNR (MSE 0.00617), (ii) a Spatial Variational Autoencoder ($\beta$-VAE) achieving 27.49\,dB validation PSNR with structured latent manifold traversal, (iii) an ImageNet-adapted ResNet-18 Territorial Classifier attaining 98.10\% validation accuracy, (iv) a Class-Conditional DCGAN (cDCGAN) reaching balanced adversarial equilibrium ($D=1.672, G=1.189$) with 99.40\% intended-class recognition across 168 synthesized tiles, (v) a Class-Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM with 20-step accelerated DDIM sampling) converging to 0.0304 noise MSE, and (vi) a domain-specific causal Transformer (MiniGPT) yielding 1.110 perplexity on municipal urban planning mandates. By delivering an end-to-end pipeline spanning signal restoration, latent anomaly mapping, multi-modal generative synthesis, and grounded policy formulation on consumer edge hardware (RTX 4050 6\,GB, 0.133\,kWh total training footprint), UrbanGen AI demonstrates that local municipal intelligence can operate reliably while preserving institutional data sovereignty.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Development of modern cities involves juggling population growth, industrial growth, transit integration, and ecology preservation~\cite{weng2002remote,batty2013new,benediktsson2015remote}. While cities grow, the traditional approach in municipal planning is based on the periodic and manual interpretation of satellite imagery along with static Geographic Information Systems (GIS) databases. Such an approach is characterized by high latencies, expensive labor force, and inability to model hypothetical future urban development or provide proactive policy-making.

Deep Learning (DL) and Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) offer new prospects in urban analytics~\cite{lecun2015deep,zhu2017deep}. The computer vision techniques perform well in land use and land cover (LULC) classification~\cite{cheng2017remote,yang2010bag,long2015fully}, while generative models such as Autoencoders (AE), Variational Autoencoders (VAE), Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), and Large Language Models (LLM) provide efficient means of signal denoising, spatial synthesis, continuous latent feature morphing, and automated policy-making. However, complex atmospheric scattering and spatial domain shift across diverse urban fabrics remain persistent obstacles~\cite{tuia2016domain}.

There are some challenges in current generative architecture in spatial domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Noisy Sensors and Degradation of Environment:} The aerial and satellite sensors are innately exposed to atmospheric scattering, cloud shadows, and optical noise in the sensor.
    \item \textbf{Non-Coherent Architecture Processes:} Analytical models such as classifiers and generative models like GANs, Variational Auto Encoders, and Transformers usually work independently of each other in a disconnected manner.
    \item \textbf{High Compute Costs and Unfriendly Environment:} Many existing generation frameworks need vast compute clusters inside data centers which makes the framework hard to implement by municipalities.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Spatial Awareness in Policy Generation:} Pre-trained off-the-shelf LLMs do not have any domain-specific fine-tuning of regional municipalities and generate infeasible policy suggestions.
\end{itemize}

These issues are addressed by UrbanGen AI, a multi-modal generative intelligence platform tailored for municipal urban spatial planning applications. The model operates on a consumer NVIDIA RTX 4050 graphics card, ensures data sovereignty, and is offered as an assistive prototype rather than an autonomous zoning authority. Claims to energy and carbon savings are only made when backed up by reproducible hardware measurements.

\section{Literature Review}
The evolution of generative artificial intelligence and deep learning across visual and spatial domains has motivated diverse architectural strategies. In multi-modal educational resource synthesis, Mane et al.~\cite{mane2026edugen} introduced EduGen, an orchestrated pipeline integrating GANs for question generation, VAEs for diagram compression, LoRA-adapted Transformers for summarization, and Diffusion models for illustration synthesis. For visual restoration and synthesis benchmarks, Mane et al.~\cite{mane2026colorization} presented a comparative analysis across VAEs, GANs, Vision Transformers, and Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), proving that iterative diffusion mechanisms achieve superior structural preservation and perceptual fidelity over adversarial and latent-variable baselines. In media security and digital forensics, Mane et al.~\cite{mane2026unified} demonstrated a unified framework coupling generative synthesis (GANs and Diffusion) with forensic detection (VAEs and Vision Transformers), establishing that multi-model systems can bridge creation and verification within a single cohesive pipeline. Addressing domain-specific data scarcity and class imbalance, Kharkate and Mane~\cite{kharkate2026skin} developed a hybrid generative framework employing VAE, DCGAN, and Diffusion models for synthetic visual augmentation to enhance diagnostic classification.

In Earth observation and remote sensing, convolutional neural networks have demonstrated high efficacy in territorial scene classification and semantic land-use parsing~\cite{cheng2017remote,yang2010bag,long2015fully}. However, spatial degradation from atmospheric scattering and sensor noise impairs downstream aerial analytics~\cite{tuia2016domain}. Early spatial restoration frameworks relied on standard denoising autoencoders~\cite{vincent2008extracting}, while deeper residual architectures~\cite{he2016deep} and multi-scale skip connections~\cite{ronneberger2015u,zhang2017beyond} have advanced feature reconstruction. In territorial classification, ImageNet-pretrained representations~\cite{krizhevsky2012imagenet} optimized via Adam~\cite{kingma2014adam} and data augmentation~\cite{perez2017effectiveness} form the standard backbone for zoning validation.

For generative visual modeling, Kingma and Welling~\cite{kingma2013auto} established Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes, later extended to $\beta$-VAE~\cite{higgins2017beta} for disentangled representations, while reconstruction disparity has been adapted for anomaly detection~\cite{an2015variational,akcay2018ganomaly}. Concurrently, Generative Adversarial Networks~\cite{goodfellow2014generative} and Deep Convolutional GANs~\cite{radford2015unsupervised} enabled sharp visual synthesis, with conditional GAN formulations~\cite{mirza2014conditional}, spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral}, projection discriminators~\cite{miyato2018cgans}, and auxiliary classifiers~\cite{odena2017conditional} stabilizing class-conditioned generation. To overcome adversarial mode collapse, non-equilibrium thermodynamics foundations~\cite{sohl2015deep} led to Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)~\cite{ho2020denoising}, with accelerated sampling enabled by DDIM~\cite{song2020denoising} and classifier guidance~\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion}. In language modeling, causal autoregressive Transformers~\cite{vaswani2017attention,radford2019language} provide powerful semantic sequence generation.

\noindent\textbf{Research Gap:} While these studies explore generative architectures in isolation or within single-modality tasks, remote sensing lacks a unified, edge-deployable multi-model platform that concurrently coordinates signal restoration, continuous latent zoning exploration, class-conditioned parcel generation, automated auditing, and grounded policy formulation. UrbanGen AI bridges this gap through a consolidated, multi-model generative intelligence pipeline.

\section{System Architecture and Methodology}
The UrbanGen AI pipeline is made up of five unique modules that tackle different stages in the spatial intelligence life cycle: restoration, classification, latent representation, generative synthesis, and contextual advisory text generation.

\subsection{Denoising Autoencoder with Skip Connections (U-Net AE)}
Degraded satellite imagery directly impairs automated zoning identification. We formulate an aerial tile denoising task where the input image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$ is corrupted by zero-mean additive Gaussian noise $\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma = 0.15$~\cite{vincent2008extracting}:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{x} = \text{clip}(x + \eta, 0, 1)
\end{equation}

The first baseline model employed a deep residual learning backbone network~\cite{he2016deep} with bottleneck compression, which generated a spatial feature map of size $8 \times 8 \times 256$. Although global textures could be recovered successfully, there was a problem of blurring and loss of high-frequency structures (important for road junctions and clustered buildings).

In order to address this issue, we added multi-scale skip connections inspired by U-Net architectures~\cite{ronneberger2015u} between the feature maps of the stem, Layer 1, Layer 2, and Layer 3 into the decoder stages via transposed convolutions:
\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = \mathcal{D}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\tilde{x}), \{\mathbf{F}_{\text{stem}}, \mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \mathbf{F}_3\}\Big)
\end{equation}
The reconstruction objective minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE), providing an effective residual learning formulation for aerial signal restoration~\cite{zhang2017beyond}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \|x^{(i)} - \hat{x}^{(i)}\|_2^2
\end{equation}

\subsection{Deep Convolutional Land-Use Classifier}
For automated municipal auditing, an ImageNet-pretrained~\cite{krizhevsky2012imagenet} ResNet-18 architecture was adapted. The final fully-connected projection layer was replaced with a 21-dimensional linear classifier corresponding to standard land-use categories:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{W}_c \cdot f_{\text{ResNet}}(x) + b_c)
\end{equation}
Trained with categorical Cross-Entropy loss over 50 epochs with Adam optimization ($\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$)~\cite{kingma2014adam} and data augmentation strategies~\cite{perez2017effectiveness}, the model performs continuous territorial zoning validation.

\subsection{Spatial Variational Autoencoder (Spatial VAE)}
While deterministic autoencoders map inputs to isolated point vectors, sustainable planning requires navigating a continuous design space to explore transitional states (e.g., transforming industrial parcels into mixed-use eco-districts).

Our spatial VAE implements a deep convolutional encoder following Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes principles~\cite{kingma2013auto} that outputs parametric distribution tensors representing mean $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$ and log-variance $\log \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$. Utilizing the reparameterization trick:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\sigma} \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})
\end{equation}

The optimization objective balances spatial reconstruction fidelity with probabilistic latent regularisation:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(x, \hat{x}) + \beta \cdot D_{\text{KL}}\big(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|x) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z})\big)
\end{equation}
Where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} = 0.7 \cdot \mathcal{L}_1 + 0.3 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}$, and $\beta = 10^{-4}$ establishes a $\beta$-VAE~\cite{higgins2017beta} reconstruction-priority regime, preventing over-regularization from smearing sharp parcel boundaries.

\textbf{Anomaly Detection Formulation:} UrbanGen AI exploits reconstruction error as an unsupervised spatial anomaly indicator~\cite{an2015variational}:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}_{\text{anomaly}}(x) = \frac{1}{C \cdot H \cdot W} \sum_{c,h,w} |x_{c,h,w} - \hat{x}_{c,h,w}|
\end{equation}
Tiles exhibiting anomaly scores exceeding three standard deviations from the empirical baseline mean are automatically flagged for physical planning inspection, mirroring semi-supervised structural anomaly frameworks~\cite{akcay2018ganomaly}.

\subsection{Class-Conditional Deep Convolutional GAN (cDCGAN)}
To augment sparse aerial datasets and generate hypothetical planning scenarios on demand, we developed a class-conditional deep convolutional generative adversarial network~\cite{goodfellow2014generative} operating at $128 \times 128$ resolution.

\noindent\textit{Architectural Taxonomy and Clarification:} Academically, this network synergistically combines two distinct paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN) Architecture}~\cite{radford2015unsupervised}: Employs strided and fractionally-strided convolutional blocks with Batch Normalization in $G$, leaky rectifiers in $D$, and avoids deterministic spatial pooling layers to stabilize spatial representation learning.
    \item \textbf{Conditional GAN (cGAN) Mechanism}~\cite{mirza2014conditional}: Conditions both the generator $G(z|y)$ and discriminator $D(x|y)$ upon class label embeddings $y \in \{1, \dots, 21\}$, directing synthesis toward specific zoning categories rather than unconstrained sampling.
\end{enumerate}
Hence, the model is a \textbf{Class-Conditional DCGAN (cDCGAN)}: its convolutional generator and discriminator provide class-conditioned aerial tile synthesis.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Generator ($G$):} Takes a latent Gaussian noise vector $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}_{128})$ concatenated with a learned class embedding $\mathbf{e}_y \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$. A dense projection block maps the 192-dimensional vector into an $8 \times 8 \times 512$ feature volume, followed by four transposed convolution blocks with Batch Normalization and LeakyReLU activations, concluding with a $\text{Tanh}$ transfer function producing $128 \times 128 \times 3$ aerial tiles.
    \item \textbf{Projection Discriminator ($D$):} Receives image $x$ through five convolutional layers stabilized with spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral} yielding deep visual representation $\phi(x)$. Adopting the Miyato \& Koyama projection mechanism~\cite{miyato2018cgans}, the adversarial score is formulated as $D(x, y) = \psi(\phi(x)) + y^T V \phi(x)$, directly inner-producting the class vector with deep spatial features, alongside an auxiliary classifier head~\cite{odena2017conditional} predicting class logits $\hat{y}$.
\end{itemize}
To ensure crisp geometric structural synthesis (e.g. tennis court boundary lines and airport runway markings), Generator training is governed by composite adversarial, auxiliary classification, and deep semantic guidance from the pre-trained ResNet-18 classifier: $\mathcal{L}_G = \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} + 0.8\mathcal{L}_{\text{AC}} + 1.2\mathcal{L}_{\text{semantic}}$. Paired with soft label smoothing ($y_{\text{real}}=0.9, y_{\text{fake}}=0.1$) and Exponential Moving Average (EMA, decay 0.999), this formulation guarantees stable convergence ($D \approx 1.401, G \approx 1.332$) without mode collapse.

\subsection{Class-Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)}
While GANs synthesize samples in a single forward pass via competitive adversarial games, they are vulnerable to training instability and mode dropping. Built upon non-equilibrium thermodynamics foundations~\cite{sohl2015deep}, UrbanGen AI incorporates a class-conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)~\cite{ho2020denoising}.

The forward diffusion process progressively perturbs clean aerial imagery $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$ over $T=1000$ discrete timesteps via a linear variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$:
\begin{equation}
q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}\Big(\mathbf{x}_t;\, \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} \mathbf{x}_0,\, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}\Big)
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$. The reverse denoising network implements a lightweight U-Net parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ conditioned on sinusoidal timestep embeddings $\mathbf{t}$ and discrete zoning class labels $c \in \{1, \dots, 21\}$. The network is optimized via the simplified noise prediction objective:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{diffusion}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, c}\Big[\big\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c)\big\|_2^2\Big]
\end{equation}
For rapid municipal decision support, synthesis accelerates via Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM)~\cite{song2020denoising}, yielding sharp aerial parcels in 20 deterministic sampling steps and demonstrating the high structural fidelity of score-based diffusion over adversarial generation~\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion}.

\subsection{Urban Plan Generator (MiniGPT Transformer)}
To bridge quantitative visual spatial analysis with qualitative policy generation, UrbanGen AI implements a custom decoder-only autoregressive causal Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017attention}. Adopting generative pre-training principles for causal language modeling~\cite{radford2019language}, the model features 4 pre-layer-normalization Transformer blocks, 4 attention heads, embedding dimension $d_{\text{model}} = 256$, and context window length $T=192$.

Trained from scratch on a domain-curated sustainable urbanism corpus (\texttt{urban\_planning.txt}) detailing transit-oriented development (TOD) paradigms, floor space index (FSI), rainwater harvesting mandates, and green buffer zoning, the model computes:
\begin{equation}
P(w_t | w_{<t}) = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} \mathbf{V}\right)
\end{equation}
Inference is executed via dynamic top-$k$ ($k=40$) and temperature-scaled sampling seeded directly with real-world municipal land-cover telemetry.

\section{Experimental Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Models were trained using PyTorch 2.x framework and evaluated using UCMerced LandUse dataset~\cite{yang2010bag} that contains 2,100 high resolution aerial tiles categorized into 21 different land uses, which include agricultural areas, dense residential areas, harbors, freeways, and storage tanks. Experiments were performed locally using NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU (6 GB VRAM, 75 W TGP). To ensure rigorous experimental comparability and eliminate batch-dependent gradient variance across all generative and analytical paradigms, a uniform batch size of 32 was maintained across all trained models.

\subsection{Land-Use Classification Accuracy}
The ResNet-18 territorial zoning classifier converged reliably on the 90/10 train/validation split across the uniform 50-epoch protocol, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:cls_metrics}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Horizon:} 50 epochs (batch size 32, Adam optimizer, $\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$).

\end{itemize}
Confusion matrix evaluation confirmed zero classification overlap between open green/agricultural land parcels and high-density urban infrastructure categories, establishing a robust automated validator for real-time municipal zoning auditing.

\subsection{Conditional Generative Synthesis (Residual cDCGAN)}
Trained under the unified 50-epoch benchmark protocol using Residual Upsampling Blocks (\texttt{ResUpBlock}), Projection Conditioning ($y^T V \phi(x)$), Spectral Normalization, diversity regularizer, and Auxiliary Classifier guidance, the cDCGAN reached balanced minimax equilibrium ($D=1.672, G=1.189$ at epoch 50). Across 168 synthesized aerial tiles spanning all 21 municipal zoning classes, the independent classifier achieved an intended-class recognition rate of 99.40\% (167 out of 168 tiles correctly recognized). In remote sensing generative evaluation, this recognition accuracy serves as an automated semantic fidelity proxy confirming that synthetic tiles exhibit the characteristic structural features of their targeted zoning category.

\section{Ethical Considerations and Governance}
Urban spatial interventions directly affect citizen well-being, mobility networks, and equitable resource allocation. Ethical guarantees are codified into UrbanGen AI as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Advisory Human in the Loop:} UrbanGen AI is designed exclusively as an assistive drafting system; automated policy or zoning enforcement without certification by a qualified urban planner is strictly prohibited.
    \item \textbf{Domain Shift Awareness and Regional Drift:} Training on standardized benchmark data introduces domain shift when applied to Global South topologies and informal settlements (e.g., in Pune). The system explicitly generates confidence metrics and diagnostic disclaimers to prevent uncritical algorithmic reliance.
    \item \textbf{Data Sovereignty and Privacy:} Operating locally on edge hardware guarantees the complete protection of high-resolution municipal spatial telemetry from third-party cloud leakage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\subsection{Conclusion}
In this paper, we present \textbf{UrbanGen AI}, a multi-model generative intelligence framework capable of deployment at the edge to facilitate data-driven municipal spatial analysis and urban planning. The design of UrbanGen AI involves the combination of six deep neural network architectures in a single pipeline, filling the gap in methodology between raw Earth observation processing and urban policy writing.

Through empirical testing on 21 classes of the UCMerced benchmark, several key architectural insights have been confirmed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Morphological Fidelity via Skip Connections:} Incorporation of multi-scale transposed skip connections in the Denoising Autoencoder led to a large improvement in reconstruction ($29.65$\,dB PSNR and $0.00449$ MSE), which allows the recovery of fine topological details like building clusters and road networks which were completely lost through the bottleneck compression of traditional autoencoders.
    \item \textbf{Structured Spatial Latent Navigation:} Preserving an explicit $8 \times 8 \times 64$ spatial tensor in the $\beta$-VAE allows for continuous, topology-preserving traversal of the latent space across different urban environments, providing a functional basis for unsupervised spatial anomaly detection and zoning compliance.
    \item Coordinated Generative Synthesis: The integration of projection-conditioned adversarial modeling (cDCGAN) and source-conditioned Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Modeling (DDPM/DDIM) is complementary in modalities for fast scenario generation and morphological visualization respectively.
     \item Grounded Policy Translations: A combination of vision-based land use analytics in combination with a causal domain-specific transformer (MiniGPT) allows automated synthesis of structural policy recommendations, providing translation from quantitative zoning statistics to municipal policies.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout this project, strict methodological limits have been adhered to: generated aerial tiles are used as scenario visualizations and not deterministic predictions in time, while anomaly scores denote deviations from statistical distributions and not independent legal judgments. Moreover, through designing the entire system to run on consumer edge hardware (NVIDIA RTX 4050 6\,GB), UrbanGen AI offers regional planners an end-to-end analytics platform that ensures local data sovereignty.

\subsection{Future Work}
Based on the strengths and weaknesses of the current prototype, a number of promising technical directions will be pursued in future research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Spectral and Multi-Temporal Satellite Feeding:} The current pipeline will be enhanced from three-channel RGB aerial images to higher-dimensional Earth observation satellites, namely 12-channel European Space Agency (ESA) Sentinel-2~\cite{drusch2012sentinel} and NASA Harmonized Landsat-Sentinel (HLS) multi-temporal surface reflectance feeds. This will include physical spectral vegetation indices (NDVI), water indices (NDWI), and thermal infrared channels for surface heat islands.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Cross-Attention Conditioning:} We intend to implement cross-modal vision-language conditioning in the diffusion architecture, enabling municipal planners to interactively condition scenario generation through joint natural language prompts, GIS vector shape files, and parcel zoning density constraints.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed Modeling of Climate and Microclimate:} In order to transform generative visualizations into physics-based environmental decision-making tools, future developments will use Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)~\cite{raissi2019physics} and CFD surrogates to model localized wind corridors, surface roughness from an aerodynamic point of view, and microclimate heat dissipation for generated structural designs.
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Change Modeling and Adaptation in the Global South:} To compensate for the absence of temporally paired data and regional distribution shift in the informal urban fabric of developing cities (for example, Pune), future benchmarks will make use of multi-temporal paired data and domain adaptive contrastive representation learning.
\end{enumerate}
