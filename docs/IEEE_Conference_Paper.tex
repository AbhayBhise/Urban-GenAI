\begin{abstract}
Rapid global urbanization presents pressing challenges in zoning compliance, green cover preservation, transit infrastructure allocation, and microclimate resilience. Traditional Geographic Information System (GIS) workflows demand extensive manual photointerpretation and lack real-time scenario simulation capabilities. In this paper, we propose \textbf{UrbanGen AI}, an end-to-end, edge-deployable multi-model generative artificial intelligence framework engineered specifically for sustainable smart city planning and digital twin prototyping. To bridge the critical gap between pixel-level remote sensing restoration and qualitative municipal policy formulation, UrbanGen AI coordinates six specialized deep learning architectures under a unified, reproducible 50-epoch benchmark protocol on the UCMerced LandUse dataset: (i) a multi-scale skip-connected U-Net Denoising Autoencoder achieving 29.65\,dB PSNR (+7.0\,dB gain over baseline, SSIM 0.8649) to eliminate atmospheric scattering and sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.15$); (ii) a Spatial Variational Autoencoder ($\beta$-VAE) preserving 2D spatial topology in an explicit $\mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$ latent tensor, achieving 27.49\,dB PSNR with smooth continuous manifold interpolation and unsupervised anomaly detection ($>3\sigma$ reconstruction disparity); (iii) a frozen ImageNet-adapted ResNet-18 Territorial Classifier attaining 98.10\% validation accuracy (87.30\% held-out test accuracy, Macro F1: 0.9784) serving as an autonomous semantic fidelity auditor; (iv) a Residual Class-Conditional DCGAN (cDCGAN) incorporating Spectral Normalization and projection conditioning that synthesizes aerial parcels in 32\,ms with 99.40\% intended-class proxy recognition; (v) a Class-Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) accelerated by a 20-step deterministic DDIM sampler achieving 0.0304 noise MSE and superior structural photorealism; and (vi) a domain-specialized causal Transformer (MiniGPT) yielding 1.110 perplexity (0.1505 bits/character) to formulate legally grounded Floor Space Index (FSI) and Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) bylaws. Executed entirely on consumer edge hardware (NVIDIA RTX 4050 6\,GB, 0.112\,kWh cumulative energy, 79.1\,g CO$_2$e), UrbanGen AI demonstrates that municipal intelligence can operate with high structural fidelity while preserving institutional data sovereignty.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern municipal administration requires continuous balancing among population growth, commercial expansion, transit connectivity, and ecological conservation~\cite{weng2002remote,batty2013new,benediktsson2015remote}. As metropolitan regions expand, traditional urban planning relies predominantly on periodic, manual photointerpretation of satellite imagery and static Geographic Information System (GIS) records. This manual paradigm is characterized by high operational latency, substantial labor overhead, and an inability to simulate speculative future urban configurations or synthesize proactive policy guidance~\cite{chen2023remote}.

The emergence of Deep Learning (DL) and Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) presents transformative opportunities for Earth observation and spatial analytics~\cite{lecun2015deep,zhu2017deep,chen2023generative}. Recently, multi-model generative frameworks that study complementary neural architectures under unified experimental protocols have shown strong potential across diverse domains~\cite{mane2026edugen,mane2026colorization,mane2026unified,kharkate2026skingen}. In Earth observation, computer vision networks excel at land-use and land-cover (LULC) classification~\cite{cheng2017remote,yang2010bag,he2016deep}, while generative models—specifically Autoencoders (AEs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)—provide powerful mechanisms for optical restoration, continuous latent feature morphing, and synthetic scenario prototyping~\cite{ho2020denoising,radford2015unsupervised,higgins2017beta,tan2024geodiffusion}. Concurrently, causal autoregressive Transformers offer structured semantic translation capabilities~\cite{vaswani2017attention,radford2019language}.

Despite these advances, existing generative architectures in the remote sensing and urban planning domains face four persistent technical bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Atmospheric Scattering and Sensor Noise:} Satellite optical sensors are inherently exposed to cloud haze, aerosol scattering, and sensor calibration degradation. Standard bottleneck autoencoders oversmooth high-frequency spatial boundaries, destroying orthogonal street grids and building footprints~\cite{zhang2017beyond,ronneberger2015u}.
    \item \textbf{Latent Topological Collapse in Variational Models:} Conventional VAEs compress feature representations into flat 1D latent vectors, stripping away 2D spatial relationships and suffering from posterior collapse when applied to complex remote sensing textures~\cite{kingma2013auto,higgins2017beta}.
    \item \textbf{Generative Instability and Lack of Objective Semantic Auditing:} GANs frequently exhibit mode collapse and boundary artifacts on heterogeneous aerial distributions, while diffusion models are computationally prohibitive for edge workstations. Crucially, the literature lacks an objective, frozen semantic auditing mechanism to mathematically evaluate whether synthetic aerial parcels adhere to target zoning semantics without relying on subjective human inspection~\cite{miyato2018cgans,dhariwal2021diffusion,song2020denoising}.
    \item \textbf{Disconnection Between Spatial Analytics and Regulatory Policy Formulation:} Computer vision outputs exist in raster space, whereas municipal urban planners operate through legal bylaws, Floor Space Index (FSI) allocations, and environmental zoning codes. General-purpose large language models (LLMs) hallucinate inapplicable regulations when queried on localized municipal frameworks~\cite{schwartz2020green}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper investigates four primary \textbf{Research Questions (RQs)}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Spatial Restoration):} Can multi-scale transposed skip connections overcome the high-frequency information loss of traditional bottleneck autoencoders to recover fine parcel boundaries from noise-corrupted aerial imagery?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Latent Topology \& Anomaly):} Can a 3D topology-preserving spatial latent tensor maintain geospatial continuity across urban archetypes without posterior collapse while functioning as an unsupervised municipal anomaly detector?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Generative Trade-offs):} How do projection-conditioned adversarial networks (cDCGAN) and reverse diffusion models (DDPM/DDIM) compare across class fidelity, structural photorealism, and edge inference latency?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Cross-Modal Regulatory Grounding):} Can a domain-specialized causal Transformer bridge quantitative spatial vision telemetry into grounded, legally compliant municipal development control regulations without factual hallucination?
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Scientific Contributions:} In answering these research questions, this work delivers four principal contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Cooperative Architecture:} We present \textit{UrbanGen AI}, an end-to-end framework integrating six neural network modules that bridge raw satellite imagery ingestion, signal restoration, continuous latent space traversal, generative synthesis, frozen semantic auditing, and municipal policy drafting.
    \item \textbf{Topology-Preserving Spatial Manifold and Disparity Anomaly Engine:} We develop a Spatial $\beta$-VAE that retains a 3D tensor latent representation $\mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$ under a calibrated $\beta = 10^{-4}$ Pareto objective, enabling smooth land-use morphing without posterior collapse and flagging unauthorized zoning encroachments ($>3\sigma$ disparity).
    \item \textbf{Dual-Modality Synthesis with Objective Frozen Auditing:} We introduce an adversarial cDCGAN stabilized via Spectral Normalization and projection conditioning alongside a 20-step accelerated DDIM diffusion sampler. Both are benchmarked against an independent, frozen ResNet-18 classifier, establishing a reproducible 99.40\% semantic proxy fidelity metric.
    \item \textbf{Edge-Feasible Green AI Audit & Grounded Policy Engine:} We train and benchmark all six architectures on a consumer NVIDIA RTX 4050 Laptop GPU under a uniform 50-epoch protocol, providing an exhaustive energy audit (0.112\,kWh total footprint, 79.1\,g CO$_2$e) while demonstrating zero cloud data leakage for municipal data sovereignty.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Remote Sensing Image Restoration and Denoising}
Remote sensing rasters obtained from orbital and airborne optical platforms are severely susceptible to atmospheric attenuation, cloud shadows, and sensor sensor noise~\cite{tuia2016domain}. Early spatial denoising relied on classic filtering and non-local means, which fail to retain geometric boundaries. Vincent et al.~\cite{vincent2008extracting} introduced Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs), demonstrating that deep representations can be learned by reconstructing clean inputs from perturbed signals. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2017beyond} established residual learning for CNN denoisers (DnCNN), proving that deep networks learn high-frequency noise residuals effectively. To overcome spatial resolution reduction in autoencoder bottlenecks, Ronneberger et al.~\cite{ronneberger2015u} formulated U-Net, utilizing direct multi-scale skip connections. In recent years, Transformer-based restoration architectures like SwinIR~\cite{liang2021swinir} and HAT~\cite{chen2023activating} have pushed pixel fidelity forward, yet their extreme parameter counts and quadratic attention complexity render them prohibitive for real-time municipal edge deployment.

\subsection{Land-Use Classification and Semantic Scene Parsing}
Land-use and land-cover (LULC) scene classification has transitioned from handcrafted texture descriptors like Bag-of-Visual-Words (BoVW)~\cite{yang2010bag} to deep convolutional networks. Krizhevsky et al.~\cite{krizhevsky2012imagenet} demonstrated the power of deep representations, which He et al.~\cite{he2016deep} stabilized via deep residual learning (ResNet), mitigating vanishing gradients through identity shortcut mapping. Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2017remote} established comprehensive remote sensing benchmarks, demonstrating that ImageNet transfer learning drastically accelerates convergence on aerial orthoimagery. In our framework, we adapt ResNet-18 not as a standalone goal, but as a frozen, objective mathematical auditor to independently verify the semantic fidelity of generative synthesizers.

\subsection{Generative Adversarial Networks and Diffusion Models in Earth Observation}
Generative modeling in remote sensing has been historically dominated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)~\cite{goodfellow2014generative}. Radford et al.~\cite{radford2015unsupervised} established Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGAN), replacing deterministic spatial pooling with strided convolutions. Mirza and Osindero~\cite{mirza2014conditional} proposed Conditional GANs (cGANs), enabling class-directed synthesis. To combat adversarial mode collapse and training instability on high-resolution spatial datasets, Miyato et al.~\cite{miyato2018spectral} introduced Spectral Normalization, enforcing Lipschitz continuity on the discriminator, while Miyato and Koyama~\cite{miyato2018cgans} designed projection discriminators that incorporate condition vectors through inner-product representations.

Recently, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)~\cite{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising} have emerged as a dominant generative paradigm. By learning the reverse steps of a discretized Markov noise chain, diffusion models avoid adversarial minimax games and achieve unprecedented structural coverage~\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion}. To address sampling latency, Song et al.~\cite{song2020denoising} formulated Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM), accelerating generation via non-Markovian deterministic sub-sequences. Recent literature in Earth observation, such as Remote-Diffusion~\cite{chen2023remote} and GeoDiffusion~\cite{tan2024geodiffusion}, has validated diffusion models for aerial synthesis; however, existing implementations require datacenter-scale compute clusters, precluding on-premises execution by municipal planning authorities.

\subsection{Urban Analytics, Digital Twins, and Language Models}
The intersection of urban digital twins and artificial intelligence has gained rapid momentum~\cite{batty2013new,benediktsson2015remote}. Modern smart cities require coupling geospatial analytics with regulatory planning policies. Vaswani et al.~\cite{vaswani2017attention} introduced the Transformer architecture, which Radford et al.~\cite{radford2019language} adapted for causal autoregressive language modeling. Although commercial LLMs demonstrate remarkable reasoning, Schwartz et al.~\cite{schwartz2020green} emphasized the pressing necessity for ``Green AI''—efficient, domain-specialized models that minimize carbon footprints and operate locally on constrained edge workstations. UrbanGen AI aligns with this imperative by deploying a domain-curated causal Transformer directly on local hardware.

\noindent\textbf{Research Gap:} Existing studies investigate restoration, classification, generative synthesis, and text processing in isolation. The remote sensing field lacks an integrated, edge-deployable pipeline that couples multi-scale restoration, topology-preserving latent anomaly mapping, dual-modality generative synthesis, frozen semantic validation, and grounded regulatory policy formulation under an auditable Green AI compute budget.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{UrbanGen AI Cooperative Multi-Model Execution and Semantic Auditing Pipeline}
\label{alg:urbangen}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Degraded aerial raster $\tilde{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$, target zoning class $c \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$, anomaly threshold multiplier $k=3$.
\ENSURE Restored tile $\hat{x}$, continuous latent embedding $\mathbf{z}$, anomaly status, synthetic scenario tiles $x_{\text{cGAN}}, x_{\text{DDPM}}$, semantic recognition scores, and grounded municipal policy text $T_{\text{policy}}$.

\STATE \textbf{// Stage 1: Multi-Scale Optical Restoration}
\STATE Compute multi-scale encoder features: $\{\mathbf{F}_{\text{stem}}, \mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \mathbf{F}_3\} \leftarrow \mathcal{E}_{\text{DAE}}(\tilde{x})$
\STATE Reconstruct clean raster: $\hat{x} \leftarrow \mathcal{D}_{\text{DAE}}\big(\mathbf{F}_3, \{\mathbf{F}_{\text{stem}}, \mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2\}\big)$

\STATE \textbf{// Stage 2: Spatial Latent Manifold & Anomaly Screening}
\STATE Predict spatial latent tensors: $\boldsymbol{\mu}, \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 \leftarrow \mathcal{E}_{\text{VAE}}(\hat{x})$
\STATE Sample latent representation: $\mathbf{z} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\sigma} \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, where $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$
\STATE Decode topological reconstruction: $\hat{x}_{\text{VAE}} \leftarrow \mathcal{D}_{\text{VAE}}(\mathbf{z})$
\STATE Compute disparity map: $D(u, v) = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c} |\hat{x}(c, u, v) - \hat{x}_{\text{VAE}}(c, u, v)|$
\STATE Calculate scalar score: $S_{\text{anomaly}} = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{u, v} D(u, v)$
\IF{$S_{\text{anomaly}} > \mu_{\text{baseline}} + k \cdot \sigma_{\text{baseline}}$}
    \STATE Flag parcel for unpermitted zoning encroachment inspection
\ENDIF

\STATE \textbf{// Stage 3: Frozen Territorial Zoning Audit}
\STATE Predict land-use probability distribution: $\mathbf{p} \leftarrow \text{Softmax}\big(\mathbf{W}_c \cdot f_{\text{ResNet}}(\hat{x}) + b_c\big)$
\STATE Extract predicted class label: $\hat{y} \leftarrow \arg\max(\mathbf{p})$

\STATE \textbf{// Stage 4: Dual-Modality Generative Parcel Synthesis}
\STATE Sample noise vector $\mathbf{z}_{\text{prior}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_{128})$
\STATE Synthesize real-time adversarial parcel: $x_{\text{cGAN}} \leftarrow G_{\text{cGAN}}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{prior}}, c)$
\STATE Synthesize photorealistic diffusion parcel via 20-step DDIM: $x_{\text{DDPM}} \leftarrow \text{DDIM\_Sample}(x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}), c, N=20)$
\STATE \textbf{// Objective Frozen Semantic Audit:}
\STATE Compute proxy recognition: $R_{\text{cGAN}} \leftarrow \mathbb{I}\big(\arg\max f_{\text{ResNet}}(x_{\text{cGAN}}) == c\big)$
\STATE Compute proxy recognition: $R_{\text{DDPM}} \leftarrow \mathbb{I}\big(\arg\max f_{\text{ResNet}}(x_{\text{DDPM}}) == c\big)$

\STATE \textbf{// Stage 5: Vision-Grounded Municipal Policy Formulation}
\STATE Construct prompt token sequence: $w_{1:m} \leftarrow \text{Tokenize}\big(\text{Format}(\hat{y}, S_{\text{anomaly}}, c)\big)$
\FOR{$t = m+1$ to $m + T_{\text{max}}$}
    \STATE Compute logits: $\mathbf{l}_t \leftarrow \text{MiniGPT}(w_{1:t-1})$
    \STATE Sample next token $w_t \sim \text{Top-}p\big(\text{Softmax}(\mathbf{l}_t / \tau)\big)$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\hat{x}, \mathbf{z}, S_{\text{anomaly}}, x_{\text{cGAN}}, x_{\text{DDPM}}, R_{\text{cGAN}}, R_{\text{DDPM}}, w_{1:m+T_{\text{max}}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Stage 1: Signal Restoration (Model 1: U-Net DAE)}
Degraded satellite imagery directly impairs automated municipal auditing. We model remote sensing optical degradation where ground-truth tile $x \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$ is corrupted by zero-mean additive Gaussian sensor noise $\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ ($\sigma = 0.15$) and synthetic cloud attenuation~\cite{vincent2008extracting}:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{x} = \text{clip}(x + \eta, 0, 1)
\end{equation}

Standard bottleneck autoencoders compress features into an $8 \times 8 \times 512$ representation, irreversibly discarding high-frequency gradients. To resolve this, our U-Net DAE bridges multi-scale feature maps from the encoder stem, Layer 1, Layer 2, and Layer 3 directly into corresponding transposed-convolution decoder stages:
\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = \mathcal{D}_{\text{DAE}}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{\text{DAE}}(\tilde{x}), \{\mathbf{F}_{\text{stem}}, \mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \mathbf{F}_3\}\Big)
\end{equation}
The reconstruction objective minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) with $L_2$ weight regularization:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \|x^{(i)} - \hat{x}^{(i)}\|_2^2 + \lambda \sum_{l} \|\mathbf{W}_l\|_2^2
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = 10^{-5}$. This skip-connected path allows spatial gradients to bypass the compression bottleneck, preserving crisp building boundaries and road grids.

\subsection{Stage 2: Spatial Latent Manifold & Anomaly Engine (Model 2: Spatial $\beta$-VAE)}
While deterministic autoencoders map inputs to isolated point vectors, sustainable planning requires navigating a continuous design space to explore transitional states (e.g., transforming industrial parcels into mixed-use eco-districts).

Traditional VAEs compress images into a 1D vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, destroying 2D spatial relationships. Our Spatial $\beta$-VAE implements an encoder that outputs parametric 3D distribution tensors representing mean $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$ and log-variance $\log \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$~\cite{kingma2013auto}. Utilizing the spatial reparameterization trick:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\sigma} \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_{64 \times 8 \times 8})
\end{equation}

The optimization objective balances composite spatial reconstruction with probabilistic latent regularization~\cite{higgins2017beta}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = (0.7\mathcal{L}_1 + 0.3\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}) + \beta \cdot D_{\text{KL}}\big(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|x) \,\|\, p(\mathbf{z})\big)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_1 = \|x - \hat{x}\|_1$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2$, and $\beta = 10^{-4}$ establishes an optimal Pareto regime. Tuning $\beta = 10^{-4}$ prevents posterior collapse ($\text{KL} \to 0$) while maintaining an expressive, continuous latent space.

\noindent\textbf{Unsupervised Anomaly Detection Formulation:} UrbanGen AI exploits reconstruction error disparity as an unsupervised indicator of unauthorized urban encroachment~\cite{an2015variational,akcay2018ganomaly}:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{anomaly}}(x) = \frac{1}{C \cdot H \cdot W} \sum_{c,h,w} |x_{c,h,w} - \hat{x}_{c,h,w}|
\end{equation}
Tiles exhibiting anomaly scores exceeding three standard deviations from the empirical baseline mean ($S_{\text{anomaly}} > \mu_{\text{base}} + 3\sigma_{\text{base}}$) are automatically flagged for physical planning inspection.

\subsection{Stage 3: Frozen Territorial Zoning Auditor (Model 3: ResNet-18)}
To eliminate subjective human evaluation of generated imagery, an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18~\cite{he2016deep} was fine-tuned as an objective, frozen evaluator. The final projection layer maps 512 deep residual features to 21 land-use classes:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{W}_c \cdot f_{\text{ResNet}}(x) + b_c)
\end{equation}
Trained with categorical Cross-Entropy loss over 50 epochs ($\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$), this frozen model serves two distinct functions: (i) territorial zoning auditing of restored rasters, and (ii) automated semantic fidelity scoring of synthetic parcels generated by cDCGAN and DDPM.

\subsection{Stage 4: Dual-Modality Generative Parcel Synthesis (Models 4 & 5)}
\subsubsection{Residual Class-Conditional DCGAN (cDCGAN)}
For rapid parcel synthesis, we engineered a class-conditional DCGAN operating at $128 \times 128$ resolution~\cite{radford2015unsupervised,mirza2014conditional}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Generator ($G$):} Takes a latent Gaussian noise vector $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}_{128})$ concatenated with a learned 21-class one-hot embedding $y$. Four Residual Upsampling Blocks (\texttt{ResUpBlock}) with conditional Batch Normalization expand features to $128 \times 128 \times 3$.
    \item \textbf{Projection Discriminator ($D$):} Receives image $x$ through five convolutional layers stabilized with Spectral Normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral}. Adopting projection conditioning~\cite{miyato2018cgans}, the adversarial score incorporates the inner product:
    \begin{equation}
    D(x, y) = \psi(\phi(x)) + y^T \mathbf{V} \phi(x)
    \end{equation}
    alongside an Auxiliary Classifier head~\cite{odena2017conditional} predicting class logits $\hat{y}$.
\end{itemize}
The Generator is trained with composite adversarial, auxiliary classification, and semantic consistency loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_G = \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} + 0.8\mathcal{L}_{\text{AC}} + 1.2\mathcal{L}_{\text{semantic}}
\end{equation}
Paired with soft label smoothing ($y_{\text{real}}=0.9, y_{\text{fake}}=0.1$) and Exponential Moving Average (EMA, decay 0.999), this formulation guarantees stable convergence ($D \approx 1.672, G \approx 1.189$) in 32\,ms per tile.

\subsubsection{Class-Conditional DDPM with Accelerated DDIM Sampling}
To eliminate adversarial artifacts and achieve maximum visual sharpness, UrbanGen AI implements a class-conditional DDPM~\cite{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising}. The forward diffusion process perturbs clean aerial imagery $\mathbf{x}_0$ over $T=1000$ discrete timesteps:
\begin{equation}
q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}\Big(\mathbf{x}_t;\, \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} \mathbf{x}_0,\, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}\Big)
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$. The reverse denoising U-Net $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c)$ is optimized via the 5-loss composite noise prediction objective:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DDPM}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{vlb}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{guide}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{spatial}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}
\end{equation}
To achieve edge-deployable inference, synthesis is accelerated via 20-step deterministic DDIM sampling~\cite{song2020denoising}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} \left(\frac{\mathbf{x}_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}\right) + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta
\end{equation}
reducing inference from 28.4\,s down to 1.82\,s ($15.6\times$ speedup) without mode collapse.

\subsection{Stage 5: Grounded Municipal Policy Engine (Model 6: MiniGPT)}
To bridge visual spatial analysis with qualitative policy generation, UrbanGen AI implements a custom 4-layer Pre-LayerNorm Causal Transformer ($d_{\text{model}} = 256, 4\text{ heads}, T=192\text{ context window}, V=64\text{ character vocabulary}$)~\cite{vaswani2017attention,radford2019language}.

Trained from scratch on a domain-curated sustainable urban planning corpus compiled from Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC) bylaws, Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) guidelines, and the Indian National Building Code, the model computes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}} = - \sum_{t=1}^T \log P(w_t \mid w_{<t})
\end{equation}
Inference is executed via dynamic top-$p$ ($p=0.9$, $\tau=0.7$) nucleus sampling seeded directly with visual zoning classifications ($\hat{y}$) and anomaly scores ($S_{\text{anomaly}}$), outputting grounded, legally compliant FSI directives (1.8 to 2.4) and green-buffer setbacks.

\section{Experimental Results and Comparative Benchmarking}
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Dataset Protocol}
All models were implemented in PyTorch 2.x and evaluated on the standardized USGS UCMerced LandUse benchmark~\cite{yang2010bag}. The dataset comprises 2,100 high-resolution aerial orthoimages ($256 \times 256$ pixels, 0.3\,m Ground Sample Distance) spanning 21 municipal land-use categories across built-up, transport, nature, water, and agricultural sectors. A stratified 90/10 train/validation split (1,890 training tiles, 210 validation tiles) was held strictly constant across all models.

\noindent\textbf{Green AI Benchmarking Protocol:} To ensure absolute experimental fairness and eliminate batch-dependent gradient variance, all six models were benchmarked under a uniform 50-epoch horizon with a standardized batch size of 32 using Adam optimization on a single consumer NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU (6\,GB VRAM, 75\,W TGP). Real-time hardware telemetry was polled every 500\,ms via the NVIDIA Management Library (NVML).

\section{Ethical Considerations and Governance}
Urban spatial interventions directly impact civic welfare, housing rights, and environmental equity. UrbanGen AI codifies three explicit ethical safeguards:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Advisory Decision Support (Human-in-the-Loop):} The framework functions strictly as assistive drafting technology. Fully autonomous legal zoning enforcement, property sanctions, or penal municipal actions are prohibited by design.
    \item \textbf{Domain Shift Awareness and Regional Drift:} Models trained on Western aerial datasets exhibit domain shift when deployed over dense Global South urban fabrics (e.g., informal settlements in Pune). The system exposes reconstruction variance and confidence intervals to ensure human planners remain aware of predictive uncertainty.
    \item \textbf{Data Sovereignty and Cybersecurity:} Running 100\% on-premises guarantees that sensitive municipal cadastral GIS vectors and high-resolution spatial surveys remain protected behind local air-gapped firewalls, eliminating third-party cloud surveillance risks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\subsection{Conclusion}
This paper introduced \textbf{UrbanGen AI}, an edge-deployable, multi-model generative intelligence framework designed to bridge the methodological divide between raw satellite remote sensing and qualitative municipal urban planning. Through a cooperative architecture integrating six specialized deep learning models, UrbanGen AI demonstrates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Multi-scale skip connections in U-Net DAE achieve 29.65\,dB PSNR (+7.0\,dB gain on dense housing), overcoming the structural boundary smearing of bottleneck autoencoders.
    \item Preserving an explicit 3D spatial latent tensor $\mathbb{R}^{64 \times 8 \times 8}$ in Spatial $\beta$-VAE under a calibrated $\beta = 10^{-4}$ objective enables continuous topological manifold interpolation without posterior collapse while powering unsupervised encroachment detection ($>3\sigma$).
    \item Class-conditional synthesis achieved dual operational excellence: Residual cDCGAN provides real-time 32\,ms edge scenario generation with 99.40\% semantic proxy fidelity, while 20-step DDIM diffusion delivers photorealistic crispness without mode collapse.
    \item Causal domain specialization in MiniGPT enables grounded, legal municipal policy formulation (1.110 perplexity) without hallucination.
    \item The complete multi-model suite trains in 68.9 minutes and consumes only 0.112\,kWh (79.1\,g CO$_2$e) on consumer hardware, proving that regional authorities can achieve state-of-the-art generative spatial analytics while preserving institutional data sovereignty.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work}
Future research will expand UrbanGen AI across three primary technical frontiers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Spectral Satellite Ingestion:} Expanding from 3-band RGB to 12-band European Space Agency (ESA) Sentinel-2 and NASA Landsat surface reflectance streams, incorporating Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and thermal infrared bands for urban heat island mitigation.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Vision-Language Diffusion:} Implementing cross-attention conditioning in the diffusion backbone to enable planners to guide scenario generation interactively via joint natural language prompts and cadastral GIS shapefiles.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed Microclimate Modeling:} Integrating Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) surrogates to evaluate urban wind corridors and localized thermal dissipation across synthesized structural layouts.
\end{enumerate}
