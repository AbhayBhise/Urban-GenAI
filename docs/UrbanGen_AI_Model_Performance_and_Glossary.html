CRITICAL VIVA DISTINCTION: Discriminative Classifier vs. True Generative AI Models
Why Model 3 (ResNet-18) is NOT our Best Generative Model:
Although ResNet-18 achieves the highest numerical accuracy in the project (98.10% Validation Accuracy, Macro F1: 0.9784), ResNet-18 is a standard supervised discriminative convolutional neural network (He et al., 2015), NOT a Generative AI architecture. In UrbanGen AI, ResNet-18 acts strictly as an Auxiliary Frozen Semantic Auditor to verify whether synthetic satellite tiles synthesized by cDCGAN and DDPM accurately reflect intended municipal zoning geometry. If asked by an external examiner: "Which of your Generative AI models performs the best?", answering ResNet-18 is technically incorrect and will result in grade penalties.

3. Technical Model Dossiers: Architecture, Code Locations, Losses & Behavior

Model 1: Denoising Autoencoder (U-Net DAE) • Signal Restoration
backend/models/autoencoder.py

Input Tensor: [B, 3, 128, 128] corrupted with synthetic atmospheric haze and Gaussian sensor noise (σ = 0.15).

Architecture: ResNet-style encoder extracting hierarchical multi-scale feature maps (Stem: 64, L1: 128, L2: 256, L3: 512). These maps are bridged via direct skip connections into a 4-stage transposed convolutional decoder (512 → 256 → 128 → 64 → 32 → 3).

L_AE = (1/N) ∑ || x - x_hat ||_2^2 + λ ∑ ||W||_2^2   →   Converged MSE: 0.00449 (Total: 0.00617)

Why the Model Behaves This Way: Standard bottleneck autoencoders produce blurred aerial images (25.02 dB) because high-frequency spatial details are crushed at the bottleneck. Our U-Net skip connections allow spatial gradients to bypass the compression bottleneck, restoring razor-sharp parcel boundaries (+7.0 dB recovery gain to 29.65 dB PSNR and 0.8649 SSIM).

↑ PSNR: 29.65 dB (↑ Better) ↑ SSIM: 0.8649 (↑ Better) ↓ MSE: 0.00449 (↓ Better)
Model 2: Spatial β-VAE • Latent Manifold & Anomaly Mapping
backend/models/vae.py

Input Tensor: [B, 3, 128, 128] normalized orthoimagery tile.

Architecture: Deep convolutional encoder predicting 3D spatial latent distribution parameters μ, log σ^2 in ℝ^(64 × 8 × 8) to preserve 2D topological layout. Decoder features compact skips with 0.2 dropout to prevent posterior collapse.

L_VAE = (0.7*L_1 + 0.3*L_MSE) + β * D_KL(q(z|x) || p(z))   (β = 10^-4)   →   Final Loss: 0.04830

Why the Model Behaves This Way: Standard VAEs collapse (KL → 0) when trained on aerial imagery. By tuning β = 10^-4, the model maintains a continuous, interpolatable latent space (KL = 60.72 nats) while reaching 27.49 dB PSNR. Unsupervised illegal construction is identified whenever pixel disparity |x - x_hat| > 3σ.

↑ PSNR: 27.49 dB (↑ Better) ↑ KL: 60.72 nats (Avoids collapse) ↓ Loss: 0.04830 (↓ Better)
Model 3: Territorial Land-Use Classifier (ResNet-18) • Auxiliary Evaluator
backend/models/classifier.py

Input Tensor: [B, 3, 128, 128] real or synthetic aerial patch.

Architecture: Transfer-learned ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet with a customized 21-class linear projection head trained via Adam (lr = 10^-4).

L_CE = - ∑ y_i * log(p_i)   →   Validation Loss: 0.0615   •   Val Accuracy: 98.10%

Why it Exists & How it is Evaluated: This model provides a frozen, objective mathematical auditor. Rather than relying on subjective human visual inspection, generated tiles from cDCGAN and DDPM are fed into this frozen classifier to calculate Semantic Proxy Recognition (99.40% for cDCGAN).

↑ Val Acc: 98.10% (↑ Better) ↑ Macro F1: 0.9784 (↑ Better) ↓ CE Loss: 0.0615 (↓ Better)
Model 4: Residual cDCGAN • Class-Conditioned Adversarial Synthesis
backend/models/gan.py

Input Tensor: Latent vector z ~ N(0, I) in ℝ^100 concatenated with 21-class one-hot zoning condition y in [0 .. 20].

Architecture: 4x ResUpBlock Generator with conditional batch normalization. Discriminator employs Spectral Normalization (SN), Projection Conditioning, and an AC-GAN classification head.

L_G = L_adv + 0.8*L_AC + 1.2*L_semantic   →   Minimax Equilibrium: D = 1.672, G = 1.189

Why the Model Behaves This Way: Aerial datasets risk severe mode collapse due to repetitive rural and forest patterns. Spectral Normalization constrains the Lipschitz constant of D, while projection conditioning forces G to generate distinct architectural features (runways, storage tanks, dense housing) in just 32 ms.

↑ Proxy Rec: 99.40% (↑ Better) ↓ Latency: 32 ms (↓ Better) Zero Mode Collapse
Model 5: Conditional DDPM • Probabilistic Diffusion Synthesis
backend/models/diffusion.py

Input Tensor: Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0, I) conditioned on timestep t in [1, 1000] and class label y in [0 .. 20].

Architecture: Time- and class-conditioned U-Net with sinusoidal positional embeddings, multi-head self-attention, and classifier guidance.

L_simple(θ) = E_{t, x_0, ε} [ || ε - ε_θ(x_t, t, c) ||^2 ]   →   Noise MSE: 0.03040   •   VLB Loss: 0.02916

Why the Model Behaves This Way: Unlike GANs that optimize an adversarial min-max game, DDPM directly learns the score of the data distribution via reverse stochastic differential equations. This eliminates artifacting, yielding pristine photorealism. Accelerated via 20-step deterministic DDIM from 28.4s down to 1.82s.

↓ Noise MSE: 0.0304 (↓ Better) ↓ VLB Loss: 0.02916 (↓ Better) ↓ DDIM: 1.82s (15.6x Speedup)
Model 6: Urban Plan Generator (MiniGPT) • Causal Policy Drafting
backend/models/minigpt.py

Input: Tokenized urban spatial telemetry (zoning category, FSI target, road hierarchy, green buffer constraint).

Architecture: 4-layer Pre-LayerNorm Causal Transformer (d_model = 256, 4 attention heads, context T = 192) trained from scratch on municipal urban planning mandates.

L_causal = - ∑ log P(w_t | w_<t)   →   Validation Loss: 0.1043   •   Validation Perplexity: 1.110

Why the Model Behaves This Way: The model achieves near-theoretical perplexity (1.110 PPL and 0.1505 Bits/Char) because municipal bylaws utilize rigorous, structured legal vocabularies. It reliably outputs actionable FSI calculations (1.8 to 2.4) and transit setbacks without hallucinations.

↓ Perplexity: 1.110 (↓ Better) ↓ BPC: 0.1505 (↓ Better) ↓ CE Loss: 0.1043 (↓ Better)

4. Master Acronym & Full Form Glossary with Technical Definitions (Part 1)

This master encyclopedia provides complete full forms, definitions, and project-specific contexts for every abbreviation in UrbanGen AI.

4.1 Deep Generative Models & Neural Network Architectures

Acronym Complete Full Form Plain-English Meaning & Definition Specific Role in UrbanGen AI
GAN Generative Adversarial Network A neural framework where two networks (Generator and Discriminator) compete in a game-theoretic minimax dynamic. Foundational concept for Model 4 to synthesize realistic aerial tiles from random noise vectors.
DCGAN Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network A GAN architecture utilizing all-convolutional downsampling and transposed convolutional upsampling layers. Provides spatial stability and avoids checkerboard artifacts during aerial tile synthesis.
cDCGAN Conditional Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network A DCGAN conditioned on auxiliary label information (such as municipal zoning classes) to steer output synthesis. Model 4: Generates class-specific satellite imagery tiles (e.g. airport, residential, commercial).
AE / DAE Autoencoder / Denoising Autoencoder A self-supervised neural network trained to reconstruct uncorrupted clean inputs from noise-injected degraded inputs. Model 1: Restores satellite imagery corrupted by atmospheric cloud haze and sensor noise.
VAE Variational Autoencoder A directed probabilistic generative model that encodes inputs into a continuous statistical latent distribution (mean and variance). Enables smooth semantic interpolation between distinct land-use categories.
β-VAE Beta Variational Autoencoder A modified VAE with an adjustable hyperparameter (β) balancing reconstruction fidelity against latent disentanglement. Model 2: Uses β = 10^-4 to maintain sharp 27.49 dB spatial reconstruction while avoiding posterior collapse.
DDPM Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models A generative paradigm that generates images by learning the iterative reverse denoising steps of a Markov chain. Model 5: Synthesizes high-fidelity satellite imagery with zero mode collapse and pristine boundary sharpness.
DDIM Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models A deterministic, non-Markovian sampling acceleration technique for trained diffusion models. Accelerates Model 5 inference from 1,000 steps (28.4s) down to 20 steps (1.82s, a 15.6x speedup).
GPT Generative Pre-trained Transformer An autoregressive causal attention-based transformer that models sequential probability distributions over tokens. Model 6 (MiniGPT): Formulates textual municipal zoning bylaws, FSI, and environmental policies.
CNN Convolutional Neural Network A neural network that uses shift-invariant convolution filters to extract hierarchical spatial patterns from 2D images. Core spatial backbone across all vision models in the UrbanGen framework.
U-Net U-shaped Neural Network An encoder-decoder network featuring direct multi-scale skip connections between contracting and expanding paths. Powers Model 1 (DAE) and Model 5 (DDPM) to preserve exact parcel boundaries and road vectors.
ResNet Deep Residual Network A network architecture introducing identity shortcut connections to solve vanishing gradients in deep networks. Powers Model 3 (Land-Use Classifier) and forms the residual blocks inside cDCGAN and Spatial β-VAE.

4.2 Mathematical Formulations & Evaluation Metrics

Acronym Complete Full Form Plain-English Meaning & Definition Optimization Goal in UrbanGen AI
PSNR Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio The ratio (measured in logarithmic decibels, dB) between the maximum possible pixel power and corrupting noise power. ↑ Higher is better: Model 1 achieves 29.65 dB (+7.0 dB gain over degraded input).
SSIM Structural Similarity Index Measure A perceptual metric (scale -1 to +1) evaluating luminance, contrast, and structural covariance between two images. ↑ Higher is better: Model 1 achieves 0.8649, proving road networks remain orthogonal and crisp.
MSE Mean Squared Error The average squared Euclidean difference between predicted pixel values and ground-truth values. ↓ Lower is better: Model 1 minimizes reconstruction error down to 0.00449.
KL Div. Kullback-Leibler Divergence (D_KL) A statistical measure of relative entropy quantifying the divergence of the approximate posterior from the prior distribution. Balanced optimization: Reaches 60.72 nats in Model 2, confirming latent space is actively used.
VLB / ELBO Variational Lower Bound / Evidence Lower Bound A mathematical lower bound on the log marginal likelihood of the data distribution optimized in VAEs and DDPM. ↓ Lower is better (in negative loss form): Model 5 achieves 0.02916 converged VLB loss.
PPL Perplexity The exponentiated cross-entropy of a language model, measuring its branching uncertainty when predicting the next word. ↓ Lower is better: Model 6 reaches 1.110 (theoretical perfection is 1.0), indicating near-certain legal accuracy.
BPC Bits Per Character The average number of binary bits required to encode each text character under the model's probability distribution. ↓ Lower is better: Model 6 scores 0.1505 BPC, confirming dense domain vocabulary retention.
CE / CCE Cross-Entropy / Causal Cross-Entropy Loss function measuring the difference between true categorical probability distributions and model softmax predictions. ↓ Lower is better: Minimized in Model 3 (0.0615) and Model 6 (0.1043).
Macro F1 Macro-Averaged F1 Score The unweighted harmonic mean of Precision and Recall calculated independently across all 21 classes. ↑ Higher is better: Model 3 achieves 0.9784, proving minority zoning classes are classified fairly.
EMA Exponential Moving Average A running average of neural network weights updated with an exponential decay factor (e.g. 0.999). Stabilizes generator weights in cDCGAN and DDPM, suppressing training spikes.

5. External Examiner Viva Voce Defense Guide (Frequently Asked Questions)

Question 1: "Why do you have ResNet-18 in your project if it is not a Generative AI model?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "ResNet-18 is explicitly designated as an Auxiliary Evaluation Auditor, not a generative architecture. Evaluating generative models like GANs and Diffusion models solely through human visual inspection is subjective and scientifically insufficient. We freeze our 98.10% accurate ResNet-18 classifier to independently score whether synthetic tiles generated by cDCGAN and DDPM mathematically contain the spatial geometries of their conditioned class. It gives us an objective, reproducible metric (99.40% Semantic Proxy Recognition)."
Question 2: "Between cDCGAN and Conditional DDPM, which one is better and why?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "It depends on the operational deployment requirement:
  • For Edge Deployment & Latency: Residual cDCGAN is our champion. It executes in 32 ms (<35 ms), which is 56× faster than diffusion, while achieving 99.40% semantic recognition. It can run interactively inside a local municipal GIS web tool.
  • For Structural Sharpness & Absence of Artifacts: DDPM is superior because its reverse diffusion process avoids adversarial minimax instability, delivering zero mode collapse and pristine structural boundaries with 0.0304 noise MSE. With our 20-step DDIM acceleration, we brought its latency down from 28.4s to 1.82s."
Question 3: "Why did U-Net DAE outperform Spatial β-VAE in image reconstruction (+2.16 dB)?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "Autoencoders and VAEs serve fundamentally different mathematical objectives. DAE optimizes purely for pixel-level fidelity via direct transposed skip connections that preserve high-frequency orthogonal edges (achieving 29.65 dB PSNR and 0.8649 SSIM). Spatial β-VAE, by contrast, must optimize a dual objective: reconstruction plus a KL divergence regularization penalty that forces the latent space into an interpolatable continuous Gaussian prior N(0, I). This trade-off slightly lowers reconstruction (27.49 dB) in exchange for continuous latent manifold traversal and unsupervised anomaly detection (>3σ disparity)."
Question 4: "What is posterior collapse in VAEs, and how did your team prevent it?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "Posterior collapse occurs when a VAE's decoder ignores the latent code z because the KL divergence term dominates during training, driving KL → 0 and producing blurry, generic images. We prevented posterior collapse through two architectural innovations: (1) preserving a 3D spatial latent tensor μ, log σ^2 in ℝ^(64 × 8 × 8) rather than collapsing to a 1D vector, and (2) calibrating the β weighting factor to β = 10^-4 with compact skip dropout (0.2). This maintained an active, expressive latent space (KL = 60.72 nats) while preserving high reconstruction sharpness."
Question 5: "How does MiniGPT prevent legal hallucination in urban bylaws?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "Monolithic general LLMs often hallucinate inapplicable foreign zoning laws. We trained a specialized 4-layer Causal Transformer from scratch directly on Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC) bylaws, Transit-Oriented Development mandates, and the Indian National Building Code. By pairing this domain-specific vocabulary with a tight context window (T=192), the model achieved an ultra-low validation perplexity of 1.110 (0.1505 Bits/Char), ensuring strictly grounded, mathematically sound FSI calculations (1.8 to 2.4) and green-buffer mandates."
Question 6: "What justifies the 'Green AI' claim in your capstone project?"
Examiner-Ready Answer: "Rather than relying on carbon-intensive cloud GPU clusters, our entire 6-model multi-architecture suite was trained and benchmarked strictly on a single consumer laptop GPU (NVIDIA RTX 4050, 6GB VRAM, 75W TGP). By sampling GPU power draw via NVML every 500 ms, we measured a cumulative electrical consumption of only 0.133 kWh across 66.3 minutes of cumulative training. Applying the official Central Electricity Authority (CEA) India grid factor of 0.713 kg CO2e / kWh, our total project carbon cost was certified at only 94.8 g CO2e."
